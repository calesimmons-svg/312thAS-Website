No-Go Requests

Policy below, and the forms are at the bottom.

The updated No-Go Policy memo is currently being routed. The AMC countermeasure program was updated this month. In the meantime, we will continue to follow the current policy memo.

BOTTOM LINE: No-Go requests will be requested in writing from the AC and should comply with AMC's Fatigue Countermeasure Program. The minimum information requested is used to comply with that reference and for the safety of prescription ordering.

Background

Although the policy memo doesn't specifically state that the request needs to be in writing, it was left somewhat vague to allow last-minute requests for no-notice missions and is not meant for interpretation. It gives the Flight Surgeons leeway in the event a verbal request is needed for mission needs. Ultimately, ALL prescriptions are at the discretion of the Flight Surgeon.

Verbal requests should only be made for a no-notice mission with less than 72 hrs notice. The Flight Surgeon can still require a written request with less than 72 hrs notice. It is the AC's responsibility to make sure the request is necessary for the mission, is emailed to the appropriate medical personnel more than 72 hrs before the mission, and that an MFR is signed by the Commander if the AC needs to pick up medications for all crew members.

No-Go pills are not to be generically issued for all missions. They should only be issued/used when other routine sleep hygiene methods can't be used (e.g., inadequate sleep/rest cycle, or sleep schedule changing by more than 4 hrs per stop). The number of pills requested is based on the specifics of the mission, and that justification should be in the request. Use your SAFTE-FAST tool and review Appendix 5 and Appendix 8.

AMC Pharmacological Fatigue Countermeasure Program

Per the AMC Pharmacological Fatigue Countermeasure Program (dated 4 September 2024):

"Non-pharmacological fatigue countermeasures (e.g. scheduling, aircrew augmentation, strategic napping, circadian rhythm shifting, caffeine, etc.) are the preferred methods of combating fatigue and making operational risk management decisions. The use of pharmacologic countermeasures for combating fatigue are an additional fatigue mitigation strategy available in consultation with the Wing/Installation Chief of Aerospace Medicine (SGP), or deployed equivalent, when a fatigue risk remains despite command's best efforts."

Approval to use no-go medications in lieu of normal sleep hygiene because of mission parameters is at the discretion of line leadership. However, issuing no-go pills is solely at the discretion of the prescribing Flight Surgeon. These are controlled substances. The DEA monitors these prescriptions and audits prescribers, and improper issuance can impact a prescriber's license and a member's health. Just as importantly, Airmen using these medications can become physically and/or psychologically dependent on them and use them to the exclusion of non-pharmacologic countermeasures. They must be prescribed with caution.

To summarize: unless the mission notice is less than 72 hrs, a written request by the AC is required so the operational need can be documented in the medical record. It is at the Flight Surgeon's discretion whether verbal requests can be made with less than 72 hrs notice. The Flight Surgeon can deny No-Go prescriptions at their clinical discretion.

What the AC's Request Must Include

No-Go requests are made by the AC and include, at a minimum:

  • Full name of crew members requesting
  • Date of birth
  • DoD ID
  • Mission number
  • AC name
  • Duration of mission
  • Number of planned crew rests
  • Number of requested no-go pills

It's suggested that requests include justification (e.g., a mission stop alters the sleep schedule by more than 4 hrs).

No policy will ever override a Flight Surgeon's clinical decision making. If you have concerns with this policy, direct them to our Chief of Aerospace Medicine, Col Vermilion.

Operational Medication Request Process: Criteria

Criteria for a No-Go request for missions:

  1. Must have completed initial ground testing.
  2. Received No-Gos within the last year AND had face-to-face counseling by the FS (annual PHA).
  3. May only request one TCON fill every 20 days.
  4. Maximum of 5 tabs and no refills. Other requests require FS approval FIRST.
  5. No history of alcohol or drug abuse.
  6. No altered mental status.
  7. No recent significant changes to health status.

Operational Medication Request Process: Instructions

  1. Receive MFR request from AC.
  2. Verify the patient has ground trialed the requested medication (cleared) and is not DNIF in ASIMS.
  3. Verify in the MHS Genesis medication list their last No-Go request.
    1. Must have completed face-to-face with FS within the last year (Fly PHA).
    2. May NOT have more than 20 pills in the last 60 days.
    3. Review documentation (Flight Medicine encounters).
  4. Open new TCON for patient — To: USAF Travis Flight Surgeon On Call, High priority.
  5. Communicate request through the scripted AutoText (all No Refills):
    1. Ambien – 5mg (female) or 10mg (female or male)
    2. Sonata – 5 tabs
    3. Restoril – 5 tabs
  6. Have the patient review FS safety & usage restrictions. If the member has questions, refer them to the FS.
  7. Once medication is approved and ordered, forward to pharmacy with timeline request; call the pharmacy (423-7657) to verify the full request.
  8. Notify the member when medication will be ready for pick-up.

MHS-G AutoText

Name, date of birth and allergies verified. Successful ground test verified without problems.
(SQ) crew member on flight orders for a multi-day mission crossing multiple time zones. Member requests operational no-go pill fill.

S/he has been given authorization by aircraft commander, --, to use no gos for jet lag, during this mission
S/he has not exceeded in permissible number of no gos in the last 60 days
S/he understands the med has an expiration date, and cannot use them past that date

Crew duty restrictions have been reviewed:
- Ambien - no crew duties within 6 hrs of ingestion
- Sonata - no crew duties within 4 hrs of ingestion
- Restoril - no crew duties within 12 hrs of ingestion
Do not take for more than 7 (10 for Sonata) consecutive days or more than 20 (28 for Sonata) pills in 60 days due to increased risk of insomnia.
This medicine will aid in gaining adequate sleep, and assists in adjusting to new time zones, but does not create circadian rhythm synchrony.
No alcohol within 12 hours of anticipated use of any of the "no-go" pills.
IAW AMC policy the member may receive Ambien 10mg #20/60 days for operational use without CC letter.

Member has been briefed by Flight Surgeon on appropriate fatigue management strategies and the role of "no-go" pills as option of last resort.
